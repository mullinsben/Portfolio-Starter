Situation

Where things stood

Set the scene. What was the landscape, what was broken, and why did it matter now? It’s not a status update—it’s the tension the rest of the deck resolves.

Because this study is gated, you can be more candid than on the public site—real context, real stakes, the messy bits. The gate is convenience, not a vault.

Name the stakes first—everything after lands harder.

Task

What I was on the hook for

State the mandate in one tight paragraph: the goal, the constraints, and the bar you were measured against. It’s not “help out with a project”—it’s a specific outcome you owned, with your name on it.

  • The objective — the single result everything else served.
  • The constraints — time, budget, headcount, politics; whatever made it hard.
  • Your remit — what was yours to decide versus what you influenced.

Action

The approach

Open with the strategy—the call that set the direction. It’s not what everyone expected; it’s the angle that made the rest possible. Say why you bet on it, and what you were willing to give up to do so.

One idea per slide keeps the deck moving. When a beat needs its own screen—give it one. That’s the whole point of the format.

Action

What we built

The substance—the thing itself, its scope, how far you took it. A before/after sells a turnaround faster than any sentence: show the old state, then the upgrade.

Action

The hard part

Every real story has one—the constraint everyone else flinched at. Describe what nearly sank it and what you did instead. It’s not bragging; it’s the part that proves you were actually there.

  • The obstacle — the thing that should have stopped you.
  • The move — what you changed, cut, or fought for.
  • The cost — the honest trade-off; credibility lives in the trade-offs.

Result

What changed

Land the plane with outcomes, not activity. Tie it back to the Situation: the tension you opened with, now resolved—in numbers where you have them, in plain language where you don’t.

  • [Result A] — the headline metric, restated with context.
  • [Result B] — the second-order win nobody expected.
  • What outlived it — the standard, system, or team that stuck around after you left.

End on the part you’re proudest of.
